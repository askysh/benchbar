### Command line

- `benchbar doctor` is much faster: on a bench with 41 apps `doctor --json` went from about 10 s to about 3 s on a quiet M4 (issue #147). It reads what its checks share once (one `lsof` for every port, one `brew info` for the three formulae and the install receipts of Homebrew's Cellar for what is installed, one `launchctl print`, one `git config` and one `git for-each-ref` per app), starts the slow checks (`bench version`, `brew info`, Python and Node probes, the site ping, the prerequisites of `--json`) side by side, runs the quick checks while they work, asks git about the apps several at a time, and does its text work in bash instead of starting `awk`, `sed`, `grep` and `tr` for it. The checks, their order and their messages are the same. `FL_DOCTOR_JOBS=1` runs everything one after the other.
- Stopping a doctor run (Ctrl-C or a TERM) now stops the checks it started, with the `brew`, `git` and `ruby` processes under them, and removes its temporary folder. Before, they ran on and the folder stayed.
